The Newborn's Crib

The newborn, awake

It moves its own eyes, makes its own sounds and jerks on its own. Nothing on this page tells it what to do. It does what its curiosity leads it to: whatever it is getting better at predicting.

    Its memory

    It keeps every moment as it came: here, the last 30,000, about forty minutes of its life. When now matches a moment it has lived, it expects what came next then, but only where that has proved better than its cerebellum's guess.

    This is our switch, not its. Lighting a memory shows us exactly what that moment brought, the way neuroscientists light up a memory's cells (Liu, Ramirez & Tonegawa, 2012). It changes nothing in it, and it never knows.

    About it

    Its brain is the being's own C code (brain/ob_life.c), compiled to run here unchanged, with a smaller memory. Its brainstem is restless of itself and fires when its own activity crosses threshold. Its basal ganglia lean on that, learning from dopamine that is its learning progress, not surprise. Plain surprise would trap it in front of anything random; progress lets it get bored and move on.

    Everything stays on this phone. Pictures, sounds and touches go only into its brain, here in the page. Nothing is uploaded. It remembers you between visits, in this browser on this device only.
