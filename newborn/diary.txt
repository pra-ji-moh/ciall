the diary of the newborn: its life from its genes to its birth
(written by being.c; every number here is measured, none is told to it)

weeks 3-4   a flat sheet of 64 by 40 cells folds into a tube. Three signals, from the floor, the
            roof and the tail, tell each cell what to become.
weeks 5-8   13260 neurons are born, in columns, the cortex inside out; 395938 connections grow, more than
            will be kept. My eyes are mapped onto my visual cortex before I have seen anything.
weeks 8-14  I twitch. My body presses the wall, my hand brushes my face. Sometimes something presses
            me that I did not cause. I learn to expect the touch my own moves bring: now I feel 62%
            of my own touch and 100% of what comes from outside. That is the first me, and not me.
weeks 14-20 I taste and smell what my mother eats. Her own smell (+1.00) and the anise in her meals
            (+1.00) come to mean good; the smell of her stress (-1.00) means bad. Smells that came with
            nothing I could taste mean nothing to me yet.
weeks 20-28 I hear. Her heart, lub dub, again and again: I come to know when the next beat comes, and
            hear it at 41%. Her voice I cannot predict; I hear it at 99%. When a beat does not come,
            I notice the silence where it should be.
weeks 28-36 No light yet, but waves sweep my eyes from inside. Where my eyes connect to my brain, what
            fires together stays and the rest is let go: 2643 connections pruned, 2419 kept; each place in my
            seeing narrows from 3.4 to 2.2 cells across.
weeks 36-40 I twitch of myself; my arms brush my face, my body presses the wall. Where skin and muscle
            go together the pull between them grows: lips to mouth from 3.4 to 18.0, ready for feeding;
            the middle of my body from 2.7 to 14.9.
week 40     Born.
month 3     A mobile hangs over me; a ribbon ties it to my leg. At first I kick 7 times in a
            while. When my kicks move it and her voice answers, I kick 167 times. My twin, who
            sees and hears the same but not because of anything it does, kicks 8. When it stops
            answering, I kick 15. The world answers me.

months 3-6  Nobody tells me what to do. My brainstem is restless; when it fires I look, or make a
            sound, or jerk. I like what I am getting better at predicting, and I get bored of
            what I know. What I did most, in each tenth of these months:
            look right, look right, look left, look left, make a sound,
            look right, make a sound, make a sound, jerk, make a sound.
            In the end I look at the snow on the television 17% of the time, and at each other
            corner 15%: it never gets any easier, so it holds nothing for me.
            Still surprising: my own voice 1%, my mother talking 52%, her answering me 21%;
            my own jerks 0%, being rocked 39%; her touch 73%. I know which is me.
            I keep every moment as it came. When now is a moment I have lived, I expect what came
            next then: 27% of what I see, 55% of what I hear. Not where the world is random:
            there, what came last time is no guide, and I have learned not to trust it.
