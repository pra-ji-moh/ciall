the diary of the newborn: its life from its genes to its birth
(written by being.c; every number here is measured, none is told to it)

weeks 3-4   a flat sheet of 64 by 40 cells folds into a tube. Three signals, from the floor, the
            roof and the tail, tell each cell what to become.
weeks 5-8   13284 neurons are born, in columns, the cortex inside out; 561742 connections grow, more than
            will be kept. My eyes are mapped onto my visual cortex before I have seen anything.
weeks 8-14  I twitch. My body presses the wall, my hand brushes my face. Sometimes something presses
            me that I did not cause. I learn to expect the touch my own moves bring: now I feel 54%
            of my own touch and 100% of what comes from outside. That is the first me, and not me.
weeks 14-20 I taste and smell what my mother eats. Her own smell (+1.00) and the anise in her meals
            (+1.00) come to mean good; the smell of her stress (-1.00) means bad. Smells that came with
            nothing I could taste mean nothing to me yet.
weeks 20-28 I hear. Her heart, lub dub, again and again: I come to know when the next beat comes, and
            hear it at 1%. Her voice I cannot predict; I hear it at 91%. When a beat does not come,
            I notice the silence where it should be.
weeks 28-36 No light yet, but waves sweep my eyes from inside. Where my eyes connect to my brain, what
            fires together stays and the rest is let go: 2132 connections pruned, 1983 kept; each place in my
            seeing narrows from 3.4 to 2.0 cells across.
weeks 36-40 I twitch of myself; my arms brush my face, my body presses the wall. Where skin and muscle
            go together the pull between them grows: lips to mouth from 3.8 to 18.9, ready for feeding;
            the middle of my body from 2.7 to 14.3.
week 40     Born.
month 3     A mobile hangs over me; a ribbon ties it to my leg. At first I kick 7 times in a
            while. When my kicks move it and her voice answers, I kick 179 times. My twin, who
            sees and hears the same but not because of anything it does, kicks 9. When it stops
            answering, I kick 12. The world answers me.

months 3-6  Nobody tells me what to do. My brainstem is restless; when it fires I look, or make a
            sound, or jerk. I like what I am getting better at predicting, and I get bored of
            what I know. What I did most, in each tenth of these months:
            look up, look up, make a sound, make a sound, make a sound,
            make a sound, make a sound, jerk, make a sound, make a sound.
            In the end I look at the snow on the television 8% of the time, and at each other
            corner 14%: it never gets any easier, so it holds nothing for me.
            Still surprising: my own voice 1%, my mother talking 54%, her answering me 17%;
            my own jerks 3%, being rocked 65%; her touch 94%.
            I know my own voice from hers. I know my own jerks from her rocking.
            All of it in my own brain: my cerebellum's synapses to my thalamus learned what to
            expect, my basal ganglia's synapses to my brainstem what to want. And I keep every
            moment: when now is one I have lived, I expect what came next then (28256 times, in
            what I heard).
