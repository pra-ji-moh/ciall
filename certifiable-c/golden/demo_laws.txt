LAWS: REACHING PAST WHAT WAS SEEN, AND SAYING SO

What it has seen: one thing added to piles of up to 30; two piles of
up to 15 pushed together; up to 7 groups of up to 7; staircases of up
to 10 steps. Each was already discovered as a concept, as a table.

THE LAWS BEHIND THE TABLES
    combine(a, 0) = a;  each step: one more
    agrees with all 256 witnessed cases
    repeat(a, 0) = 0;  each step: combine with the same amount again
    agrees with all 64 witnessed cases (some checks leaned on an earlier law past what it saw)
    stairs(0) = 0;  each step: combine with the next count
    agrees with all 11 witnessed cases (some checks leaned on an earlier law past what it saw)
  Addition is one more, again and again. Multiplication is addition,
  again and again. A staircase is adding the next step. Found, in that
  order, each resting on the one before.

INSIDE WHAT IT HAS SEEN
  combine(7, 8) = 15
      seen, not inferred
  repeat(6, 7) = 42
      seen, not inferred

PAST ANYTHING IT HAS SEEN
  combine(40, 70) = 110
      resting on:
        - one more is always the next number, past 30 too
        - combining works the same past 15
  repeat(12, 13) = 156
      resting on:
        - one more is always the next number, past 30 too
        - combining works the same past 15
        - repeating works the same past 7 groups of 7
  stairs(100) = 5050
      resting on:
        - one more is always the next number, past 30 too
        - combining works the same past 15
        - staircases grow the same way past 10 steps

A PATTERN IN THE STAIRS
  two staircases of n steps side by side: is that something it knows?
    matches repeat(n, n + 1) on all 11 staircases it has seen
    matches repeat(n + 1, n) on all 11 staircases it has seen
    (8 candidates tried, 2 matched)
  at 1000 steps, computed the long way: 1001000 and 1001000, equal
  That is Gauss's rule, found. It holds everywhere it was checked, and
  agrees at 1000. It is still NOT proved for every n: that takes proof by
  induction over the laws themselves, the next stage. Until then it is a
  conjecture, and called one.
