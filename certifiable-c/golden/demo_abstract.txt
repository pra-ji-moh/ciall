BUILDING ITS OWN WORLDS FROM RAW OBSERVATION

A BALANCE. Seven raw yes/no attributes: a weight on each of six pegs,
and whether the beam is painted red. It sees all 128 situations and
which way the beam goes. Nothing says what "left" or "count" means.
    left peg 1         matters
    left peg 2         matters
    left peg 3         matters
    right peg 1        matters
    right peg 2        matters
    right peg 3        matters
    beam painted red   never matters (proved)
  interchangeable: {left peg 1, left peg 2, left peg 3}  -> invents: how many of these are on (0 to 3)
  interchangeable: {right peg 1, right peg 2, right peg 3}  -> invents: how many of these are on (0 to 3)
  worlds: 128 raw situations seen, 16 worlds in what it built; its questions DETERMINE the outcome
  and then, over its own questions, the rule (rows: how many in the first
  group, columns: how many in the second; L tips left, R tips right):
       = R R R
       L = R R
       L L = R
       L L L =
  It found "more" as well as "how many", and the paint never mattered.

SIX LAMPS, and a chime that rings when an even number are lit
    lamp 1             matters
    lamp 2             matters
    lamp 3             matters
    lamp 4             matters
    lamp 5             matters
    lamp 6             matters
  interchangeable: {lamp 1, lamp 2, lamp 3, lamp 4, lamp 5, lamp 6}  -> invents: how many of these are on (0 to 6)
  worlds: 64 raw situations seen, 7 worlds in what it built; its questions DETERMINE the outcome
  the chime, by how many are lit: 0:rings 1:- 2:rings 3:- 4:rings 5:- 6:rings
  64 raw situations became 7 worlds, and "even" is a fact about those 7.

THE BALANCE AGAIN, but it only ever sees 90 of the 128 situations
    left peg 1         matters
    left peg 2         matters
    left peg 3         matters
    right peg 1        matters
    right peg 2        matters
    right peg 3        matters
    beam painted red   never seen to matter (dropped: a conjecture)
  interchangeable (conjectured): {left peg 1, left peg 2, left peg 3}  -> invents: how many of these are on (0 to 3)
  interchangeable (conjectured): {right peg 1, right peg 2, right peg 3}  -> invents: how many of these are on (0 to 3)
  worlds: 90 raw situations seen, 16 worlds in what it built; its questions DETERMINE the outcome
  the 38 situations it never saw, through the worlds it built:
    38 inferred; checked against what really happens: 38 right, 0 wrong
    0 with no grounds (no seen situation in the same world): no answer
  Each inference rests on the conjectures above and says so. It got from
  90 cases to the ones it never saw by finding WHAT to generalise over.

THREE SWITCHES, and a light that comes on when they spell a prime
number in binary (switch 1 worth 4, switch 2 worth 2, switch 3 worth 1)
    switch 1           matters
    switch 2           matters
    switch 3           matters
  no two attributes can be swapped without changing what happens, so
  there is nothing to count. Every raw distinction matters on its own.
  on its own: {switch 1}
  on its own: {switch 2}
  on its own: {switch 3}
  worlds: 8 raw situations seen, 8 worlds in what it built; its questions DETERMINE the outcome
  Place value is an abstraction, but not one that comes from symmetry,
  and this stage only finds those. It says so instead of forcing one.
